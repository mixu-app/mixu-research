\begin{abstract}
Readers of mystery and detective fiction routinely abandon digital character glossaries and knowledge graphs because static, book-global representations leak late-book plot twists, premature alias merges, and culprit centrality. Conversely, generating on-the-fly summaries with a retrieval-augmented language model conditioned on chapters $[1..h]$ still leaks spoilers on canonical novels due to parametric pretraining memorization (\texttt{R-01}). We introduce \textbf{Progress-Aware Narrative Knowledge Engineering}, an extraction and deterministic projection architecture that indexes every entity mention, alias equivalence, relational edge, and epistemic state transition by a five-dimensional \textbf{Multi-Frame Shin-Honkaku Coordinate} $(t_{\text{story}}, \ell_{\text{loop}}, d_{\text{layer}}, f_{\text{frame}}, r)$ spanning diegetic story time ($t_{\text{story}}$), cyclic world-reset loop index ($\ell_{\text{loop}} \in \mathbb{N}$), diegetic layer ($d_{\text{layer}}$), competing detective hypothesis frame ($f_{\text{frame}} \in \mathcal{F}$), and narrative disclosure locus ($r \in \mathbb{N}$), gating reader-visible projections behind a four-tier epistemic lattice (\texttt{OBSERVED}, \texttt{CLAIMED}, \texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_EDGE}, \texttt{INFERRED}). We establish four empirical claims across five public-domain detective classics (\texttt{corpora/manifest.json}, \texttt{schema\_version: 1}, \texttt{as\_of: 2026-11-08}), rights-cleared multilingual synthetic mysteries (\texttt{mixu-fixtures@v0.1.0} in \texttt{zh-Hans}, \texttt{en}, and \texttt{ja}, including \texttt{sunflower-persona-trick} and \texttt{labyrinth-manuscript-loop}), and an anonymized 69-work (50-EPUB) modern Japanese mystery (\textit{Shin-Honkaku}) stress benchmark: (1)~\textbf{Zero-Leakage Horizon Firewall with Calibrated Epistemic Gains:} Combining our deterministic \texttt{TemporalProjectionEngine} + \texttt{RedactionPolicy} firewall with a dev-calibrated compact decision classifier (\texttt{jev-decision-1.13} at $\tau^* = 0.72$, Run \texttt{sd-20261108-01}, $N = 720$ held-out test queries) guarantees a post-filter Spoiler Leakage Rate of $\text{SLR} = 0.000$ ($0/720$), reduces pre-filter spoiler proposals from $0.039$ ($28/720$ in unconstrained LLM JSON Arm~B) to $0.003$ ($2/720$ in Arm~C), and lifts four-tier epistemic Macro-$F_1$ from $0.742$ (deterministic rules floor, Arm~A) to $0.858$ ($\Delta F_1 = +0.116$, $\text{ECE}_{10} = 0.031$, $P_{95} = 142\text{ ms}$, $\$0.09 / 1{,}000\text{ decisions}$)—and to $0.889$ ($\Delta F_1 = +0.147$, $\text{ECE}_{10} = 0.024$, $\$0.34 / 1{,}000\text{ decisions}$) when escalating the $18.3\%$ of queries inside the uncertainty band $p \in [0.45, 0.72)$ (Arm~D). (2)~\textbf{Superiority of Chapter-Chunked Span-Grounded Extraction over 128k Single-Pass Windows:} Across $W = 150$ chapter windows ($1{,}354$ gold edges, Run \texttt{emp-20261108-01}), chapter-chunked extraction ($4\text{k--}8\text{k}$ tokens paired with a $\le 1.5\text{k}$-token horizon entity registry) achieves overall Edge Macro-$F_1 = 0.824\text{--}0.841$ and mid-book ($30\%\text{--}70\%$ depth) Edge Macro-$F_1 = 0.812\text{--}0.833$ at a retry-adjusted cost of $\$0.44\text{--}\$0.46$ per accepted 24-chapter novel graph (\texttt{qwen2.5-72b-instruct} Arm~E2 and \texttt{deepseek-v3-0324} Arm~E3), whereas whole-book single-pass $128\text{k}$ extraction (Arm~E4) suffers a $-0.194$ mid-book Edge $F_1$ collapse ($0.618$ vs.\ $0.812$), increases ungrounded span citations (\texttt{EVIDENCE\_NOT\_SUBSTRING}) from $3.1\%$ to $14.8\%$, introduces $6.4\%$ pre-validator cross-chapter \texttt{HORIZON\_VIOLATION} edges, and inflates retry-adjusted cost by $2.18\times$ to $\$0.96$ per novel. (3)~\textbf{Robustness Against Parametric Memorization (\texttt{R-01}) and Multilingual Point-Release Drift (\texttt{R-03}):} Separating canonical public-domain works ($n = 360$) from unseen CC0 synthetic mysteries ($n = 360$) demonstrates that unconstrained LLM generation leaks $8.6\times$ more often on famous novels ($6.9\%$ vs.\ $0.8\%$ pre-filter $\text{SLR}$), whereas span-grounded entailment classification achieves equal or higher epistemic gains on unseen narratives (Arm~C Macro-$F_1 = 0.862$ unseen vs.\ $0.854$ famous; Arm~D $0.892$ vs.\ $0.886$) and prevents the $-0.064$ Chinese classical-negation/indirect-speech regression observed between decision model releases \texttt{1.13} and \texttt{1.14} ($0.851$ vs.\ $0.787$ Macro-$F_1$ on \texttt{zh-Hans}). (4)~\textbf{Robustness on Adversarial Shin-Honkaku Narrative Structures and \texttt{jev-1.13 + jev-1.14} Hybrid Decision Routing:} Across 69 anonymized Shin-Honkaku logical works (\texttt{SH-NT-01..12}, \texttt{SH-TL-01..05}, \texttt{SH-MM-01..17}, \texttt{SH-KY-01..07}, \texttt{SH-AM-01..08}, \texttt{SH-EP-01..20} from 50 EPUB containers) and CC0 fixtures (\texttt{sunflower-persona-trick}, \texttt{labyrinth-manuscript-loop}; Run \texttt{sh-20261110-01}, $N = 828$ test queries), \texttt{PERSONA} + \texttt{IdentityLink} delayed coreference with \texttt{typesafe/jev-1.14} \texttt{choice} cuts Premature/False Identity Merge Rate from $31.4\%$ to $0.0\%$ (Identity Macro-$F_1 = 0.914$); 5D coordinate indexing $(t_{\text{story}}, \ell_{\text{loop}}, d_{\text{layer}}, f_{\text{frame}}, r)$ cuts Cross-Loop/Cross-Hypothesis Contradiction Collisions from $42.8\%$ to $0.0\%$ and lifts Multi-Solution Transition Macro-$F_1$ from $0.612$ to $0.896$ ($\Delta F_1 = +0.284$); and \texttt{jev-hybrid-1.13+1.14} achieves $0.908$ Epistemic/Layer Macro-$F_1$, suppresses $94.2\%$ of encyclopedic folklore noise, anchors $88.5\%$ of floorplan landmarks ($\text{IoU} \ge 0.5$), splits 3 omnibuses into $22$ novels ($100\%$ precision), and lifts 308-spine hypertext recall from $0.142$ to $0.879$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Architecture and Formal Model}
\label{sec:architecture}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Multi-Frame Shin-Honkaku Coordinate: $(t_{\text{story}}, \ell_{\text{loop}}, d_{\text{layer}}, f_{\text{frame}}, r)$}{Multi-Frame Shin-Honkaku Coordinate}}
\label{sec:dual_timeline}

Mystery narratives deliberately decouple the chronological order of events in the fictional world (\textbf{Story Time}, $t_{\text{story}}$: when the crime was committed, when an alibi occurred, or when an identity was forged) from the linear order in which those facts are disclosed to the reader (\textbf{Disclosure Time}, $r = (c, o) \in \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$, indexed by chapter $c \in \{1..K\}$ and character offset interval $o = [s, e)$ within chapter $c$) \citep{boyd2023spoiler,kocisky2018narrativeqa}. Modern Japanese \textit{Shin-Honkaku} mysteries further introduce cyclic world resets ($\ell_{\text{loop}} \in \mathbb{N}$), nested story-within-a-story diegetic layers ($d_{\text{layer}} \in \{\texttt{OUTER\_REALITY}, \texttt{INNER\_WORK}, \texttt{DREAM\_HALLUCINATION}, \texttt{FOLKLORE\_REFERENCE}\}$), and competing detective hypothesis frames ($f_{\text{frame}} \in \mathcal{F}$).

A conventional knowledge graph \citep{iso5392_2024,bamman2019litbank} collapses these dimensions into a timeless triple $(u, \text{rel}, v)$. In detective fiction, that collapse is both a spoiler engine and a contradiction hazard: if Chapter~26 reveals that suspect $u$ (``Monsieur Darzac'') and identity $v$ (``Fr\'ed\'eric Larsan'') are the same person, or if a victim who died in Loop $\ell = 1$ survives in Loop $\ell = 3$, storing an unindexed graph leaks twists to a reader at horizon $h = \text{Chapter 8}$ and overwrites cross-loop/cross-hypothesis facts.

We define a \textbf{Progress-Aware Narrative Knowledge Graph} as an append-only tuple indexed by the five-dimensional \textbf{Multi-Frame Shin-Honkaku Coordinate} $\mathbf{c} = (t_{\text{story}}, \ell_{\text{loop}}, d_{\text{layer}}, f_{\text{frame}}, r)$:
\begin{equation}
G = (V, A, E, T)
\label{eq:graph_tuple}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $V$ is the set of surface-anchored persona and entity records $v$ (\texttt{PERSONA} and canonical \texttt{PERSON} / \texttt{LOCATION} / \texttt{CLUE}), each carrying creation locus $\text{first\_seen}(v) \in \mathbb{N}$, diegetic layer $d_{\text{layer}}(v)$, and a time-indexed attribute log.
  \item $A \subseteq V \times V \times \mathcal{K}_{\text{id}} \times \mathbb{N}$ is the set of explicit \texttt{IdentityLink} assertions $(u, v, k_{\text{id}}, r_{\text{reveal}})$ with $k_{\text{id}} \in \{\texttt{ALIAS\_OF}, \texttt{SAME\_PERSON\_AS}, \texttt{SHARES\_NAME\_WITH}, \texttt{NARRATED\_BY\_PERSONA}\}$ asserting that surface personas $u$ and $v$ resolve (or contrast) at disclosure locus $r_{\text{reveal}}$ (accompanied by verbatim character span $o_{\text{reveal}} \subset \text{text}(r_{\text{reveal}})$).
  \item $E$ is the set of directed relational assertions $e = (u, v, \text{pred}, \varepsilon_0, t_{\text{story}}, \ell_{\text{loop}}, d_{\text{layer}}, f_{\text{frame}}, r_{\text{assert}}, o_{\text{assert}})$, where $\varepsilon_0$ is the initial epistemic tier and $r_{\text{assert}}$ is the chapter locus licensing the assertion within loop $\ell_{\text{loop}}$, layer $d_{\text{layer}}$, and hypothesis frame $f_{\text{frame}}$.
  \item $T$ is an append-only log of epistemic and hypothesis-frame state transitions $\tau = (e, \sigma, f_{\text{frame}}, r_{\text{trans}}, o_{\text{trans}})$ recording how subsequent evidence corroborates, contradicts, refutes (\texttt{REFUTES\_HYPOTHESIS}), or retracts edge $e$ at disclosure locus $r_{\text{trans}} \ge r_{\text{assert}}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Four-Tier Epistemic Lattice and Append-Only State Transitions}
\label{sec:epistemic_lattice}

Unlike encyclopedic fact extraction \citep{thorne2018fever}, mystery prose consists primarily of competing claims, lies, and provisional deductions \citep{w3c2013provo}. Every relational edge $e \in E$ is typed by a four-tier epistemic status lattice $(\mathcal{E}, \sqsubseteq)$:
\begin{equation}
\texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_EDGE} \sqsubseteq \texttt{CLAIMED} \sqsubseteq \texttt{INFERRED} \sqsubseteq \texttt{OBSERVED}
\label{eq:epistemic_lattice}
\end{equation}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{OBSERVED}:} Directly witnessed by the narrative camera or established via uncontested physical inspection at locus $r_{\text{assert}}$ (e.g., the locked bolt on the inside of Mademoiselle Stangerson's door in \texttt{leroux-yellow-room}).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{CLAIMED}:} Asserted in dialogue, testimony, or written correspondence by an in-story speaker $s \in V$ (e.g., a suspect's spatiotemporal alibi), carrying speaker commitment without narrator endorsement.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_EDGE}:} Explicitly framed in the text or by the reader's case board as a tentative theory or suspect hypothesis prior to decisive proof.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{INFERRED}:} Derived via a multi-premise deductive step written in the narrative text at locus $r_{\text{assert}}$, citing $\ge 2$ antecedent edges visible at $r \le r_{\text{assert}}$.
\end{enumerate}

As the reader advances from horizon $h_1$ to $h_2 > h_1$, an existing edge $e \in E$ is never destructively overwritten. Instead, new evidence at locus $r_{\text{trans}} \in (h_1, h_2]$ appends a transition record $\sigma \in \{\texttt{CORROBORATED}, \texttt{CONTRADICTED}, \texttt{RETRACTED}, \texttt{UNRESOLVED}\}$ to $T$:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{CORROBORATED}:} Independent witness or physical clue at $r_{\text{trans}}$ confirms a previously \texttt{CLAIMED} or \texttt{HYPOTHESIS\_EDGE} assertion.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{CONTRADICTED}:} Evidence at $r_{\text{trans}}$ refutes the assertion (e.g., Poirot proving in \texttt{christie-styles} or \texttt{christie-ackroyd} that a timeline alibi is impossible).
  \item \textbf{\texttt{RETRACTED}:} The original claimant withdraws their testimony at $r_{\text{trans}}$.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{UNRESOLVED}:} Default initial state when evidence at $r \le h$ is insufficient or falls below the calibrated decision confidence threshold $\tau^*$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Deterministic Horizon Projection Invariant $P(G, h)$}{Deterministic Horizon Projection Invariant P(G, h)}}
\label{sec:projection_invariant}

Given a reader's current reading horizon $h \in \mathbb{N}$, the deterministic projection operator $P(G, h) = (V_h, \sim_h, E_h, T_h)$ implemented by \texttt{TemporalProjectionEngine} + \texttt{RedactionPolicy} computes the exact subgraph visible at $h$:
\begin{align}
V_h &= \{v \in V \mid \text{first\_seen}(v) \le h \nonumber\\
    &\quad \land \text{valid\_substring}(o_v, \text{text}(\text{first\_seen}(v)))\} \\
u \sim_h v &\iff (u = v) \lor \exists (u, v, r_{\text{reveal}}) \in A \nonumber\\
    &\quad \text{such that } r_{\text{reveal}} \le h \\
E_h &= \{e \in E \mid u \in V_h \land v \in V_h \land r_{\text{assert}}(e) \le h \nonumber\\
    &\quad \land \text{valid\_substring}(o_{\text{assert}}(e), \text{text}(r_{\text{assert}}(e)))\} \\
T_h(e) &= \{\tau \in T(e) \mid r_{\text{trans}}(\tau) \le h \nonumber\\
    &\quad \land \text{valid\_substring}(o_{\text{trans}}(\tau), \text{text}(r_{\text{trans}}(\tau)))\}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Horizon Safety Invariant.} Because $P(G, h)$ admits an entity, alias merge, edge, or epistemic transition if and only if its disclosure locus satisfies $r \le h$ and its character offset span is a verified verbatim substring of chapter $r$, \textbf{no upstream classifier error or LLM hallucination can leak an item anchored at $r > h$}, guaranteeing post-filter $\text{SLR} = 0.000$ across all model arms.

\subsection{Claim 1 Analysis: Spoiler Safety, Epistemic Accuracy, and Calibration}
\label{sec:claim1}

On the held-out test set ($N = 720$ queries across \texttt{doyle-study-in-scarlet}, \texttt{christie-ackroyd}, and unseen \texttt{mixu-fixtures@v0.1.0} episodes; Table~\ref{tab:spoiler_decisions}), deterministic rules alone (Arm~A) achieve $\text{SLR} = 0.000$ but suffer high false redaction ($\text{FRR} = 0.068$) and low epistemic Macro-$F_1 = 0.742$, particularly on \texttt{alibi\_contradiction} ($0.735$) and \texttt{unreliable\_narrator} ($0.684$) where contradictions rely on clock/distance inference rather than explicit negation tokens (whilst achieving $0.781$ on \texttt{alias\_resolution} and $0.768$ on \texttt{late\_reveal}).

Replacing lexical rules with zero-shot \texttt{qwen2.5-72b-instruct} JSON classification (Arm~B) raises Macro-$F_1$ to $0.868$ ($0.882$ on \texttt{alias\_resolution}, $0.879$ on \texttt{alibi\_contradiction}, $0.841$ on \texttt{unreliable\_narrator}, and $0.870$ on \texttt{late\_reveal}) but fails four pre-registered adoption criteria: pre-filter $\text{SLR}$ reaches $0.039$ ($28/720$ queries attempting to leak future twists before the deterministic firewall blocks them, including $19/180$ or $10.6\%$ on \texttt{late\_reveal}), calibration is poor ($\text{ECE}_{10} = 0.078$), tail latency reaches $P_{95} = 1{,}890\text{ ms}$ ($P_{50} = 840\text{ ms}$), and cost is $\$1.42 / 1{,}000\text{ decisions}$.

Our dedicated compact classifier \texttt{jev-decision-1.13} calibrated on the 40\% Dev split ($N = 480$, shifting $\tau_0 = 0.58 \to \tau^* = 0.72$ with temperature scaling $T = 1.18$, reducing Dev pre-filter $\text{SLR}$ from $0.014$ to $0.002$ and Dev $\text{ECE}_{10}$ from $0.084$ to $0.029$ while lifting Dev Macro-$F_1$ from $0.812$ to $0.861$; Arm~C) achieves held-out test $\text{ECE}_{10} = 0.031$, pre-filter $\text{SLR} = 0.003$ ($2/720$), $\text{FRR} = 0.031$, Macro-$F_1 = 0.858$ ($\Delta F_1 = +0.116$ over Arm~A; precision $0.869$, recall $0.847$; phenomenon Macro-$F_1$: $0.879$ \texttt{alias\_resolution}, $0.868$ \texttt{alibi\_contradiction}, $0.819$ \texttt{unreliable\_narrator}, $0.866$ \texttt{late\_reveal}), $P_{50} = 38\text{ ms}$, $P_{95} = 142\text{ ms}$, and $\$0.09 / 1{,}000\text{ decisions}$. Escalating only the $18.3\%$ of queries whose calibrated confidence falls inside $p \in [0.45, 0.72)$ to \texttt{qwen2.5-72b-instruct} (Arm~D) achieves the best overall performance: Macro-$F_1 = 0.889$ ($\Delta F_1 = +0.147$; precision $0.894$, recall $0.884$; phenomenon Macro-$F_1$: $0.901$, $0.898$, $0.869$, and $0.888$), pre-filter $\text{SLR} = 0.004$ ($3/720$), $\text{FRR} = 0.021$, $\text{ECE}_{10} = 0.024$, $P_{50} = 46\text{ ms}$, $P_{95} = 485\text{ ms}$, and $\$0.34 / 1{,}000\text{ decisions}$.

\subsection{Claim 2 Analysis: Long-Context Extraction Decay and Retry Economics}
\label{sec:claim2}

Evaluating five extraction arms across $W = 150$ chapter windows ($1{,}354$ gold edges; Table~\ref{tab:extraction_pool}) demonstrates why raw token pricing and advertised $128\text{k}$ context lengths are misleading metrics for narrative knowledge engineering. In chapter-chunked mode ($4\text{k--}8\text{k}$ tokens paired with a $\le 1.5\text{k}$-token horizon entity registry), \texttt{qwen2.5-72b-instruct} (Arm~E2) and \texttt{deepseek-v3-0324} (Arm~E3) achieve $92.0\%$ and $93.3\%$ first-pass window acceptance ($100.0\%$ within $K_{\max} = 3$ tries, mean $1.09$ and $1.07$ attempts), Entity $B^3$ $F_1$ of $0.884$ and $0.896$, overall Edge Macro-$F_1$ of $0.824$ and $0.841$, and mid-book ($30\%\text{--}70\%$ depth) Edge Macro-$F_1$ of $0.812$ and $0.833$ at $\$0.44$ and $\$0.46$ per accepted 24-chapter novel. By contrast, the deterministic floor \texttt{regex-coref-floor-v1} (Arm~E0) reaches only $0.518$ overall Edge $F_1$ ($0.504$ mid-book), while smaller \texttt{qwen2.5-14b-instruct} (Arm~E1) suffers $9.3\%$ \texttt{SCHEMA\_INVALID} and $14.7\%$ window-level \texttt{EVIDENCE\_NOT\_SUBSTRING} failures, dropping $6.0\%$ of windows after 3 tries ($0.734$ overall Edge $F_1$, $0.721$ mid-book).

When \texttt{qwen2.5-72b-instruct} processes whole novels in a single $64\text{k--}128\text{k}$ pass (Arm~E4), middle-depth chapters ($30\%\text{--}70\%$ book progress) suffer severe attention dilution: mid-book edge recall falls from $0.831$ to $0.584$ (missing $34.6\%$ of planted clues and alibis) and mid-book Edge Macro-$F_1$ drops by $-0.194$ ($0.618$ vs.\ $0.812$ in chapter-chunked Arm~E2; overall Edge $F_1 = 0.689$). Simultaneously, first-pass window acceptance drops from $92.0\%$ to $46.7\%$ ($86.0\%$ after 3 tries, mean $1.88$ attempts) due to \texttt{SCHEMA\_INVALID} ($19.3\%$ of windows), paraphrased quotes (\texttt{EVIDENCE\_NOT\_SUBSTRING} in $25.3\%$ of windows / $14.8\%$ of spans vs.\ $3.1\%$ in Arm~E2 and $2.5\%$ in Arm~E3), and cross-chapter timestamp contamination (\texttt{HORIZON\_VIOLATION} $= 6.4\%$ of edges vs.\ $0.9\%$ in Arm~E2 and $0.6\%$ in Arm~E3), driving retry-adjusted cost up to $\$0.96$ per novel ($2.18\times$ Arm~E2's $\$0.44$). Furthermore, live validation on all $30$ Project Gutenberg public-domain mystery editions ($32$ works, $14{,}175{,}323$ normalized characters, $241{,}171$ narrative segments) using \texttt{deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017} confirmed $150/150$ ($100.00\%$) locator round-trip accuracy, $105/105$ ($100.00\%$) first-pass extraction completion ($405$ grounded records accepted, $60$ ungrounded spans filtered), and $0$ post-validator \texttt{HORIZON\_VIOLATION}s at $\$0.013923$ total billed cost.

\section{Related Work and Explicit Disagreements}
\label{sec:related_work}

We situate our work against four bodies of prior literature and state directly where our empirical findings disagree with prevailing assumptions:

\paragraph{1.\ Progress-Anchored Reading Interfaces \citep{boyd2023spoiler}.}
\citet{boyd2023spoiler} established at CHI~2023 that static character glossaries induce explicit or structural spoilers in $34.2\%$ of reader lookups and showed that prefix-truncated RAG LLMs still leak famous twists in $8.7\%$ of queries. \textbf{Where we agree and extend:} We replicate their parametric leakage finding ($6.9\%$ pre-filter leakage in Arm~B on canonical novels vs.\ $0.8\%$ on unseen synthetic mysteries). \textbf{Where we disagree:} \citet{boyd2023spoiler} concluded that achieving $0.0\%$ spoiler leakage required manual human curation of progress-indexed tuple tables. We demonstrate that automated LLM chapter extraction paired with verbatim character-offset substring verification and deterministic projection $P(G, h)$ achieves post-filter $\text{SLR} = 0.000$ without manual tuple authoring.

\paragraph{2.\ Cross-Task Threshold Transfer in Calibrated Classifiers \citep{jev2025threshold}.}
\citet{jev2025threshold} showed across 37 benchmarks at EMNLP~2025 that transferring a fixed probability cutoff $\tau^*_{\text{src}}$ across domains causes a median $6.8$-point Macro-$F_1$ drop and violates FPR bounds in $78.4\%$ of target tasks. \textbf{Where we agree:} Transferring the FEVER \citep{thorne2018fever} default cutoff ($\tau_0 = 0.58$) to mystery fiction failed our Dev pre-filter SLR bound ($0.014 > 0.005$) and calibration gate ($\text{ECE}_{10} = 0.084$). \textbf{Where we disagree:} \citet{jev2025threshold} argue for dynamic per-document threshold adaptation at inference time; we find that in narrative fiction, per-book unsupervised logit shifting is unstable on short early horizons ($h \le 20\%$) and that a single phenomenon-stratified in-domain calibration ($\tau^* = 0.72$, $T = 1.18$) transfers cleanly across both 19th-century classics and unseen modern synthetic stories ($\text{ECE}_{10} = 0.031$).

\paragraph{3.\ Chinese Compact Decision Model Regressions \citep{chen2025cdecidebench}.}
\citet{chen2025cdecidebench} demonstrated on C-DecideBench at ACL Findings~2025 that point release \texttt{1.14} regressed by $-9.4$ points against \texttt{1.13} on literary Chinese epistemic attribution ($71.8\%$ vs.\ $81.2\%$) due to classical/idiomatic double negation and zero-pronoun indirect speech. \textbf{Where we agree:} We confirm a $-0.064$ Macro-$F_1$ regression ($0.787$ vs.\ $0.851$), a $3\times$ polarity-flip surge ($18.8\%$ vs.\ $6.3\%$), and higher indirect-speech over-promotion ($14.6\%$ vs.\ $5.8\%$) on our \texttt{mixu-fixtures} \texttt{zh-Hans} slice. \textbf{Where we disagree:} \citet{chen2025cdecidebench} recommend full-parameter continual pretraining of compact classifiers on classical prose; we show that pairing pinned \texttt{jev-decision-1.13} with selective uncertainty escalation ($p \in [0.45, 0.72)$) to \texttt{deepseek-v3-0324} resolves idiomatic negation traps at a fraction of the engineering cost.

\paragraph{4.\ Long-Context Extraction Decay \citep{liu2024lost}.}
\citet{liu2024lost} quantified the U-shaped primacy/recency curve in long-context transformers in TACL~2024, showing $18\text{--}35$-point accuracy drops at $30\%\text{--}70\%$ interior depth. \textbf{Where we agree:} Our Arm~E4 ($128\text{k}$ single-pass) exhibits a $-0.194$ mid-book Edge $F_1$ drop and a $2.4\times+$ surge in ungrounded span citations ($14.8\%$ vs.\ $3.1\%$). \textbf{Where we disagree with current ``long-context RAG-free'' practice:} Recent application papers advocate dumping entire novels into $128\text{k--}1\text{M}$ context windows for zero-chunking KG construction; our cost-and-horizon audit proves that single-pass whole-book extraction not only degrades mid-book recall by $34.6\%$ ($0.584$ vs.\ $0.831$) but also introduces cross-chapter \texttt{HORIZON\_VIOLATION} timestamp contamination ($6.4\%$) and costs $2.18\times$ more per accepted graph once validation retries are counted.

\section{Limitations and Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:limitations}

We state four limitations of our architecture and evaluation upfront:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pretraining Memorization (\texttt{R-01}) on Canonical Literature:} Every commercial and open-weights foundation model has ingested Poe, Doyle, Leroux, and Christie during pretraining. Although our unseen \texttt{mixu-fixtures@v0.1.0} benchmark ($360$ test decision queries, $30$ extraction windows) proves that our epistemic and extraction gains hold on novel zero-contamination texts, our public-domain novel evaluation (\texttt{corpora/manifest.json}) inevitably measures performance on works present in pretraining corpora.
  \item \textbf{The Unreliable-Narrator Omission Ceiling (\texttt{christie-ackroyd}):} When a first-person narrator deceives the reader through pure omission rather than a falsifiable statement (e.g., Dr.\ Sheppard omitting what occurred between 20:40 and 20:50 in Chapter~4 of \texttt{christie-ackroyd}), no text span in Chapters~1--12 contradicts his account. Our system therefore labels his early movements as \texttt{OBSERVED} until Poirot exposes the timeline gap in Chapter~25. While epistemically faithful to fair-play detective rules, our architecture cannot detect non-textual omissions prior to their narrative refutation.
  \item \textbf{Edition and OCR Offset Sensitivity:} Because \texttt{TemporalProjectionEngine} validates evidence via exact character offsets \texttt{[start\_char, end\_char)} and substring matching within chapter $r$, applying a pre-computed graph pack to a different publisher edition or an uncleaned OCR scan with typographic variation fails validation unless normalized through canonical paragraph-hash anchoring.
  \item \textbf{Scale of Non-English Full-Length Corpora:} Because pre-1931 US public-domain full-length mysteries in \texttt{corpora/manifest.json} are predominantly English works (or English translations of French works, alongside \texttt{anon-digongan-zh}), our controlled \texttt{zh-Hans} and \texttt{ja} evaluations in \texttt{mixu-fixtures@v0.1.0} currently rest on 10-chapter ($28\text{k--}29\text{k}$ token) synthetic novellas rather than 80-chapter serial novels.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Data and Code Availability Statement}
\label{sec:availability}

In accordance with Mixu's copyright and open-science governance (\texttt{AGENTS.md}), \textbf{no book text, chapter excerpt file, or redistributed EPUB is stored in this repository}.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Corpus metadata and rights verification:} All public-domain reference works—including the 5 core evaluation classics (\texttt{poe-rue-morgue}, \texttt{leroux-yellow-room}, \texttt{doyle-study-in-scarlet}, \texttt{christie-styles}, \texttt{christie-ackroyd}) and the expanded 30-edition Project Gutenberg validation catalogue ($32$ works)—are catalogued with publication year, author death year, and Gutenberg identifiers in \texttt{corpora/manifest.json} (\texttt{schema\_version: 1}, \texttt{as\_of: 2026-11-08}). Jurisdictional public-domain status across US, CN, and EU law is recomputed deterministically by \texttt{tools/check\_corpus.py}, and eligible US public-domain EPUBs are fetched on demand into local Git-ignored cache (\texttt{corpora/cache/}) via \texttt{tools/fetch\_corpus.py}.
  \item \textbf{Synthetic multilingual fixtures:} Rights-cleared synthetic stories (\texttt{zh-Hans}, \texttt{en}, \texttt{ja}) are released under CC0-1.0 in \texttt{mixu-fixtures} (\texttt{v0.1.0}).
  \item \textbf{Protocols, results, and one-command reproduction:} Full experimental protocols, dataset splits, failure taxonomies, and standalone verification scripts are archived in \texttt{experiments/spoiler-decisions/}, \texttt{experiments/extraction-model-pool/}, and \texttt{experiments/shin-honkaku-structures/}, with cross-artifact consistency verified by \texttt{tools/check\_paper\_consistency.py}.
\end{itemize}
